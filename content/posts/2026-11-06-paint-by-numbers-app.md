You see a beautiful finished landscape in an app-store screenshot. Then you open the **paint by numbers app** and discover that completing it means hunting for tiny numbered patches. Is that the relaxing activity you wanted—or just another task?

A good choice depends on how you enjoy spending your attention. Some people love the certainty of following numbers. Others prefer picking their own colors or making brushstrokes. Before downloading several apps, learn what the experience actually involves.

## How a paint by numbers app works

A typical digital paint-by-numbers picture is divided into closed regions. Each region is labeled with a number that corresponds to a color in a palette. Select a color, find matching regions, and tap to fill them. As the regions fill, the complete image appears.

This differs from three other kinds of digital art:

- **Free coloring:** You choose which colors belong in each area.
- **Freehand painting:** You control the position, shape, and pressure of your strokes, depending on the tools.
- **Paint by numbers:** The picture supplies both the regions and the intended colors; your job is to find and fill them.

Some apps combine these approaches. The name alone does not tell you whether you can change colors, paint with a brush, or simply tap numbered cells. Check the actual interaction before deciding.

## Why the numbered approach appeals to some people

After a day full of choices, following a clear pattern can feel pleasantly straightforward. You don't need to know color theory or decide whether a shadow should be blue or violet. There is an obvious next step, and every filled region makes the image more complete.

The appeal isn't universal, though. Searching for the last tiny area marked “17” can be irritating, especially on a small screen. A completion percentage may encourage you to finish even when you would rather stop.

**The useful question is not whether paint by numbers is relaxing for everyone. It is whether you enjoy the process—not just the finished picture.**

## Seven things to test before choosing an app

Marketing screenshots usually show finished artwork. Spend a few minutes with an unfinished image instead.

1. **Number visibility:** Can you read the labels without constant zooming? If labels are hidden until you select a color, is the matching area easy to identify?
2. **Zoom and navigation:** Can you pinch to zoom and pan smoothly? Test a detailed corner, not just a large sky.
3. **Hints:** Does the app help locate the final small regions? Find out whether hints are free, limited, or tied to ads.
4. **Interruptions:** Notice when ads appear. An ad between completed pictures feels different from an interruption halfway through one.
5. **Saving progress:** Close an unfinished picture and reopen it. Is your work preserved without extra steps?
6. **Content and cost:** Count how many complete pictures you can try for free. Read subscription terms and check whether individual pictures require purchases.
7. **Accessibility:** Try the palette and numbers at your normal screen brightness. Color-only cues may be difficult for some users; clear labels and contrast matter.

You do not need every feature. Someone coloring for five minutes on a commute may value progress saving and offline access. Someone spending an hour on a tablet may care more about detailed artwork and precise zoom controls.

## Choose pictures for the process, not only the result

A spectacular image can be tedious to fill if it contains hundreds of tiny regions. Start with a picture whose complexity matches the time you have.

**For a short session:** Choose broad areas, fewer colors, and an uncomplicated subject such as a flower, cup, or simple skyline.

**For a longer session:** Try a city street, detailed landscape, or architectural scene. Repeating patterns and small windows can be satisfying if you enjoy careful searching.

**For variety:** Alternate between familiar subjects and places you have never visited. A picture of a distant town can also inspire you to look up its buildings, food, or geography afterward.

If travel scenes appeal to you, consider the ideas in [how to deal with wanderlust](/how-to-deal-with-wanderlust/). Looking at a city with curiosity can be enjoyable even when a trip is not possible.

## A ten-minute test that reveals whether it fits

Instead of judging an app by its gallery, use one picture for a short trial.

**Minute 1:** Pick an image you genuinely like, preferably a free one with medium-sized regions.

**Minutes 2–3:** Fill several large areas. Notice whether selecting colors and finding numbers feels intuitive.

**Minutes 4–6:** Zoom into smaller details. Test a hint, if available, and check whether the interface gets in your way.

**Minutes 7–8:** Put the phone down briefly. Do you want to return to the picture, or do you mainly want the progress indicator to reach 100%?

**Minutes 9–10:** Save the unfinished image and leave. Return later to see whether resuming is easy.

This trial also helps you distinguish **enjoyment from obligation**. If you dislike hunting for the last few numbered regions, there is no reason to finish them. You can switch to a simpler picture or a different creative hobby.

## Common frustrations and simple fixes

**“I can't find the remaining numbers.”** Zoom out first to scan the composition, then inspect edges and small background details. If hints are available, use them without treating that as cheating.

**“My finger covers the area I'm tapping.”** Try landscape orientation if supported, a larger device, or a compatible stylus. A stylus is optional, not a requirement.

**“The picture is too complicated.”** Choose fewer regions rather than forcing yourself to finish. Complexity is a preference, not a measure of skill.

**“I keep getting interrupted.”** Check whether the app offers a paid ad-free option and whether its price is worthwhile to you. Otherwise, try a paper coloring book or another offline activity.

**“I don't want more screen time.”** That's a perfectly good reason to use a printed paint-by-numbers sheet, pencils, or watercolor supplies instead. Digital convenience is only useful when it fits your day.

## What to look for in the long run

A paint by numbers app worth keeping should make it easy to start, pause, and return. Picture variety matters, but so do readable numbers, comfortable controls, clear pricing, and a pace you can choose for yourself.

Try one picture without a completion deadline. If filling the regions is enjoyable, keep going. If not, choose another way to make something with color. The point of a creative break is the time you spend with it—not how quickly you reach the final image.
